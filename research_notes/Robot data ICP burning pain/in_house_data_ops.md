# In-house robot data collection, teleop and data-ops teams: burning pain

Research date: 2026-11-07. Scope: hiring, spend, vendor failure points, complaints, deadlines, and the sub-segment with the most spend plus urgency.

Evidence labels: STRONG = job postings, named-buyer quotes, contracts, budgets, headcount. MEDIUM = exec interviews, reputable press. WEAK = vendor marketing, SEO/aggregator pages. Company-only claims are marked as such.

Caveat on sourcing: most job data came from aggregators (startup.jobs, ev.careers, Dice, Khosla job board, Builtin), not company pages. Only two company pages were fetched directly (Figure Greenhouse index, Foundation Rippling index), and neither showed compensation or responsibilities. Treat titles as confirmed, pay as aggregator-reported.

---

## Q1. Who is hiring for collection, teleop, data ops, data QA, or fleet ops?

### Takeaway
Figure has the largest visible in-house data-ops footprint (about 8 data-ops openings out of 49 on the first Greenhouse pages, 103 total). 1X, Agility, Foundation, Sunday and Tesla also run in-house collection with hourly operators and at least one manager-level owner each. Operator pay clusters at $20 to $46 per hour; manager pay is roughly $120k to $219k. Most hiring is in San Jose, San Carlos, Fremont, Palo Alto and Santa Clara, with Figure also staffing Mexico City, São Paulo and a UAE site.

## Q2. What do these teams say they build in-house?

### Takeaway
Figure built its own collection app (Index) and runs its own data-creator pipeline. Agility's manager posting puts station readiness, campaign planning and throughput metrics on the in-house team. 1X's postings ask operators to annotate with internal tools and improve SOPs. Specific tool names for most teams are not public.

### Cited Findings
- Figure built Index, a crowdsourced collection app, in-house after vendors reportedly fell short. Figure's claims: more than 264,000 downloads, more than 44,000 weekly active users, more than 16 million videos uploaded, and a $1B+ commitment to data and compute infrastructure. Source: [Alphasignal, Index](https://alphasignal.ai/news/figure-s-index-app-pays-44-000-people-to-train-its-humanoid-robots); [Pebblous summary](https://blog.pebblous.ai/blog/figure-index-robot-training-video-gig/en/) (secondary); index launch date Aug 25, 2026 per coverage. Company claims; not independently verified.
- Agility manager posting: owns "station and robot readiness," throughput per operator hour, usable-episode yield, rejection rate, and "rig uptime." Source: [ev.careers Agility listing](https://ev.careers/jobs/786496716/apply); [ev.careers second Fremont listing](https://ev.careers/jobs/758340062/apply).
- Sunday glove capture plus a "conversion" pipeline from glove data to robot-equivalent data (company claim: 90% success). Source: [Sunday journal](https://www.sunday.ai/journal/no-robot-data) (company claim).
- 1X operators annotate with internal tools, file issue logs, and feed SOP and equipment feedback. Source: [1X Recruitee](https://1x.recruitee.com/o/senior-operator-data-collection-day-shift).

### Inferences
- The in-house stack most likely includes: collection rig or station management, operator scheduling and staffing, episode QA and rejection, robot uptime and maintenance, and an annotation or labeling layer. Named products are unknown from public evidence.

### Gaps
- No public engineering blog from Figure, 1X, Agility, or Tesla on their collection stack was found in this pass.

## Q3. Complaints about vendors or in-house collection

### Takeaway
Named-company complaints about data vendors were not found. The strongest vendor-failure claim comes from Figure, which says vendors could not meet throughput, diversity and quality. The other evidence is general: novice operators produce demonstrations that succeed but train poorly, and operator management is a recurring quality driver.

### Cited Findings
- Figure: "vendors couldn't hit the throughput, diversity, or quality bar Helix requires, so we built the pipeline ourselves." Company statement, repeated by press. Source: [Figure via cryptobriefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/figure-ai-index-app-humanoid-robots/); [glitchwire](https://glitchwire.com/news/figure-ai-announces-index-a-crowdsourced-robot-training-dataset-that-could-resha/article.md) (MEDIUM for quote, but it is the company's own claim; no independent check).
- Independent caution on Figure's metrics: downloads and uploads are distribution numbers, not accepted training hours. Source: [explainx Figure Index analysis](https://explainx.ai/blog/figure-index-robot-dataset-august-2026) (WEAK; aggregator analysis).
- arXiv paper (May 2026): novice teleop operators often produce demonstrations that succeed at the task but are poor for training (inefficient motion, repeated corrections, operation near joint limits); high-quality demos "remain difficult to obtain in practice." Proposes post-episode feedback using telemetry. Source: [arXiv 2605.26349](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.26349) (MEDIUM; academic).
- A vendor guide calls operator management "the most underappreciated factor" in teleop data quality. Source: [roboticscenter.ai quality checklist](https://www.roboticscenter.ai/ko/blog/teleoperation-data-quality-checklist) (WEAK; vendor marketing).
- Encord advises buyers to check how a vendor's process changes as volume grows. Source: [Encord vendor guide](https://encord.com/blog/best-data-collection-services/) (WEAK; vendor marketing).
- Tesla 2024: Nvidia Research's Animesh Garg estimated the data needed would cost about half a billion dollars, with no guarantee of success. Source: [Business Insider via businessworld](https://businessworld.in/article/tesla-seeks-data-collectors-for-optimus-robots-offers-48-per-hour-for-the-job-530192) (2024; MEDIUM).
- Staffing and pay friction: hourly operators are contract or part-time at several vendors and OEMs, with listed pay ranging from $15 to $48/hr. Source: postings in Q1 above.
- Vendor speed: one vendor claims large vendors take 4 to 6 weeks for comparable scope. Source: [Labellerr / vendor claim cited in search](https://www.labellerr.com/blog/top-teleoperation-companies-humanoid-robotics/) (WEAK; vendor marketing).

### Inferences
- The "vendors fall short" pain is documented mainly by the one company that built in-house (Figure). Nobody else has publicly said it. This is a real gap for a startup to test, but it is not yet proven across buyers.
- Quality pain is operator-driven, which points toward tooling for operator training, feedback loops, and episode scoring, not just more hands.

### Gaps
- No named complaint from Scale, Mecka, Micro1, Mercor, Encord, Labelbox, or XDOF was found.
- No Glassdoor or LinkedIn posts from operators were searched; operator fatigue and turnover are unevidenced beyond posting requirements (standing 8+ hrs, lifting 50 lb, 12-hr rotations).
- Robot downtime complaints: no direct evidence found beyond Agility's "rig uptime" metric and Foundation's "log issues" duties.

## Q5. Which sub-segment shows the most spend plus urgency?

### Takeaway
Humanoid OEMs running in-house, multi-shift and multi-site collection rigs (Figure above all, then 1X, Agility, Foundation, Tesla) show the most headcount and the most urgency. Their workaround is the in-house operator-and-manager shift model itself. Foundation-model labs that pivoted to glove or video capture (Generalist, Sunday, Dyna) show less teleop headcount, with spend shifting to capture hardware and QA.

### Cited Findings
- Figure: about 8 data-ops roles on the first Greenhouse pages across San Jose, CDMX and Sao Paulo; a global data-ops expansion role; a $175k to $200k AI Data Operations Lead; and a Data Creator Manager role. Sources: see Q1.
- 1X: Senior Operator at $36 to $46/hr, Supervisor at $80k to $115k, Manager at $120k to $150k, each on rotating shifts. Source: Q1.
- Agility: Manager at $168k to $219k owning throughput, rig readiness and vendor make-or-buy decisions. Source: Q1.
- Tesla: 2024 reporting; more than 50 people in the role; $25 to $48/hr. Source: Q1 (older).

### Inferences
- Best-fit ICP for a startup selling tooling or managed ops: humanoid or manipulation OEMs with at least one in-house shift team and at least one expansion or scale-up in progress. The buyer is the data-ops or teleop manager who owns throughput, yield and rig uptime (Agility and 1X titles), and whose team is already on payroll at $36 to $46/hr per operator.
- Pain that recurs and that vendors arguably serve badly: keeping throughput and yield up across multiple shifts and sites, operator training and quality feedback (per arXiv and Figure's quality claim), and launching new sites quickly (Figure's expansion role).
- Confidence: medium. Spend and recurrence are strong from postings. Deadline and vendor-failure evidence is thin and rests on company claims.

### Gaps
- Could not verify the dollar spend on in-house teams (no budgets or contracts found).
- Could not verify whether Figure or 1X already buy vendor data at scale; Figure's claim says it stopped buying from outside vendors, but no contract data exists.
- Mecka, Scale and XDOF buyer lists were not checked against these OEMs.
