# Field Deployment Pain for Robots at Customer Sites (as of 2026-11-07)

Scope note: About 16 tool calls, WebSearch only (standard mode), no full-page fetches. Most evidence is from search snippets and aggregator pages, so quality is mostly weak-to-medium. Several named companies (Dexterity, Ambi, Plus One pricing, Covariant post-Amazon, Locus, Bear, Diligent/Moxi, Universal Robots integrators) returned almost nothing 2025-2026 specific. Those gaps are reported as gaps, not filled.

Evidence grades used: STRONG = job posting, named-buyer quote, contract, budget, headcount, earnings disclosure. MEDIUM = exec interview, reputable trade press. WEAK = vendor marketing, aggregator, SEO page.

The clearest 2026 signals are job postings that create dedicated fleet-reliability, field-service and remote-support teams (Tutor Intelligence, Symbotic, Serve Robotics, Gecko). ## 3. Time-to-deploy at a new site, contract drivers, failure modes

### Takeaway
Vendors say weeks; surveys and buyer statements say months. The best 2026 signals are a 6-12 month project span (MMH/Robotics Group survey, 166 respondents, March-April 2026), Exotec's October 2026 regret study, and a GXO statement that humanoid ROI is still years out. Deadlines come from peak season, new-site openings and contract milestones, but no 2026 source quantified deadline penalties.

### Cited Findings
- Intralogistics Robotics Survey 2026 (MMH, MHI, The Robotics Group; 166 subscribers, March-April 2026): Klappich puts a typical robotics project at 6-12 months from inception to go-live, faster than conventional automation. 29% of respondents deployed one to three years ago and are operational; 17% went from purchase to deployment in under a year. — [MMH 2026 intralogistics survey](https://www.mmh.com/article/2026_intralogistics_robotics_survey_robotics_moves_into_the_mainstream). MEDIUM (industry survey, small sample).
- Exotec survey released October 28, 2026: regret traced to three pre-implementation decisions: underestimating lifetime ownership cost, unclear accountability for issue resolution, and deploying automation before operations were ready. A buyer quote describes a multi-vendor system that "stops working as one," leaving the customer unsure who owns the fix. — [Exotec](https://www.exotec.com/?p=16188). WEAK-to-MEDIUM (vendor-sponsored survey, but the accountability-for-fixes point is directly relevant to the ICP question).
- Formic: published deployment claims are "weeks rather than months" with under 8 weeks for SL20 palletizer (2022-era) and about 12 weeks typical lead time to operation per Control Engineering; "4x faster time to deploy from signed contract" claimed in a 2025-era funding release. — [Control Engineering](https://www.controleng.com/robotics-as-a-service-expands-new-headquarters-machine-tending/); [Formic datasheet](https://formic.co/downloads/Formic-Machine-Tending-CNC-Datasheet.pdf); [Control.com](https://control.com/news/democratizing-robotic-automation-formic-releases-latest-raas-solutions/). WEAK (vendor claims), with one MEDIUM trade-press reference to 12 weeks.
- Universal Robots integrator deployments: a March 2026 Robotomated article says realistic cobot deployments take 2-8 weeks, and 800+ certified UR integrators exist globally. ABI Research says UR "struggles to find system integrators to support growth" and that end-manufacturers want to offload SI services. — [Robotomated](https://robotomated.com/learn/market/cobot-adoption-manufacturing-2026); [ABI Research](https://hs.abiresearch.com/market-research/insight/7785091-universal-robots-struggles-to-find-system). WEAK (Robotomated), MEDIUM (ABI snippet, date and methodology not confirmed).
- Deployment engineer job postings (around 20 found, mostly 2025-2026): core duty is on-site install, commissioning and integration with customer IT and security review, plus post-deployment support. Travel up to 75-100% in some postings. Pay where disclosed $60K-$140K; one Boston-area $80K-$120K. Experience asks range from 3+ to 8+ years. — [Motion Recruitment listing](https://motionrecruitment.com/tech-jobs/boston/direct-hire/robotics-deployment-engineer/773423); [Beacon Engineering listing](https://beaconengineers.catsone.com/careers/21995/jobs/16628740-Deployment-Engineer); [Rise listing for Sereact](https://joinrise.co/sereact-gmbh/robotics-deployment-engineer-us-dzt9); [Roboligent listing via search](https://www.dice.com/job-detail/733f61a5-1a01-46eb-8e2f-50337a72bd38). STRONG (postings), aggregated; the travel and IT-security points are specific.
- Lucas Systems and Interact Analysis (via Surface and Panel): payback can take two to three years; the Lucas study reports operational disruption from warehouse changes. — [Surface and Panel](https://www.surfaceandpanel.com/warehouse-robot-automation-roi/); [Pulse.bot summary of Lucas](https://www.pulse.bot/supply-chain/blogs/warehouses-pay-the-price-for-a-lack-of-agility-finds-lucas-systems-ba5389d8-1599-4c6e-98b0-132383099d14/). WEAK (secondary).
- Symbotic: 11 deployments started in one quarter, 77 in flight. The GreenBox site slipped to 60-90 days for its next go-live. — see Section 1. MEDIUM-STRONG.
- Agility/GXO: Digit expansion to two sites starts early 2027, about 15 months after the initial 2024 deployment's first commercial phase; GXO CEO says ROI is not yet proven. — see Section 1. MEDIUM-STRONG.

Failure modes that show up across sources (all MEDIUM or WEAK):
- Accountability gaps across multi-vendor stacks (Exotec buyer quote).
- Deploying before operations are ready (Exotec).
- Underestimated lifetime ownership cost (Exotec).
- Integration with customer IT and security reviews (deployment engineer postings).
- Revenue or volume shortfall tied to a partner's ramp (Serve Robotics Q2 Uber ramp removed).
- Per-deployment revenue declining (Symbotic summary).

### Inferences
- Deadlines are real but poorly documented. The best candidate drivers are new-site openings (GXO early 2027, Symbotic GreenBox go-lives), city pilot approvals (Serve Vancouver), and peak-season ramps. None of the sources here gives a dollar penalty per week of delay.
- The most painful, recurring cost that shows up in postings is integration and commissioning labor (deployment engineers, 75-100% travel) plus post-go-live reliability teams. Both are internal headcount.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the cost of a delayed site go-live, a contract penalty, or the number of weeks lost per new site.
- Outside services spend on deployment, simulation or evaluation was not found in any 2025-2026 source (see Section 4).

## 4. Outside services bought for this (teleop staffing, data, simulation, eval)

### Takeaway
Teleoperation data collection is being bought from third-party labor marketplaces (TSMG-style postings). Live exception-handling staffing from outside vendors is not visible in these sources. Simulation and evaluation were not found as a bought service for site adaptation. This is the most important gap to close with a primary interview.

### Cited Findings
- TSMG runs a steady stream of Robot Teleoperator postings for data collection and AI training across manufacturing, logistics and retail, which implies a third-party labor vendor selling teleop capacity to robot developers. — [TSMG Lever](https://jobs.lever.co/tsmg/f0a06415-8a23-4403-a85e-19612cda0e94). STRONG (postings) for the existence of demand; buyer identity not disclosed.
- Symbotic and Tutor run internal field service and remote support teams rather than outsourcing (postings). — see Section 1. STRONG for internal headcount.
- Plus One sells its remote supervision as a managed service (Yonder), i.e., outside staffing of exception handling is a product. WEAK (vendor marketing).
- Vendor-claimed exception rates and RaaS models (Formic, Agility) show outside vendors bundling deployment and support into subscription pricing. — [Automate on RaaS](https://www.automate.org/news/what-is-robotics-as-a-service-and-how-is-it-different-from-the-traditional-approach/aph). WEAK.
- Integrator capacity is constrained (ABI on UR SIs). — see Section 3. MEDIUM.

### Inferences
- Demand for outside exception-handling staffing is plausible but unproven in the sources here. Buyers keep these functions in-house (fleet and reliability teams). The outsourced layer that exists is data-collection teleop.
- Simulation and eval tooling was not surfaced at all in 2025-2026 buyer evidence in this search. Treat that as a gap, not a finding of absence.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of a buyer paying a third party for live exception handling by the hour or per robot at a named customer.
- No 2026 evidence on simulation or eval spend for site adaptation.

## 5. Sub-segment with most spend, deadline pressure and poor vendor service

### Takeaway
Based on this search, the strongest combined signal is in delivery and sidewalk robotics plus large warehouse operators with multi-site rollouts (GXO, Symbotic, Serve): they run dedicated reliability and field teams, post steady roles, and face revenue or site-timeline pressure. Evidence of vendors serving them badly is anecdotal (Exotec quote, GXO ROI skepticism). Evidence of spend on an outside workaround is weak. No sub-segment shows a clear, documented outside-vendor spend on exception handling. Confidence is low.

### Cited Findings
- Delivery operators with fleet-maintenance teams and a stated uptime metric (Serve Chicago posting, Tutor fleet reliability role). — see Section 1. STRONG (postings).
- Warehouse operators with multi-site rollouts and named deadlines (GXO early 2027; Symbotic GreenBox 60-90 days). — see Section 1. STRONG/MEDIUM.
- Vendor-poor service signal: a buyer says a multi-vendor system "stops working as one" and nobody owns the fix. — [Exotec](https://www.exotec.com/?p=16188). WEAK-MEDIUM.
- Economics skepticism from a named buyer: GXO has not yet achieved ROI on humanoids. — see Section 1. MEDIUM.
- Cobot integration: integrator capacity is short (ABI). — MEDIUM.

### Inferences
- The most plausible ICP for a site-adaptation, demo-collection or remote-assist startup is a multi-site warehouse or delivery operator running a robot fleet from a third-party vendor, with an in-house reliability team that is already growing (Tutor-style postings show headcount is being built), and a deadline tied to a new-site opening. Such a buyer is paying for internal headcount now. The unmet need is shared accountability and speed to resolve exceptions across vendors.
- Weakest-evidence segments: hospitals, food service, agriculture and retail. Nothing in this search surfaced a 2025-2026 posting, earnings disclosure or contract for those. Not researched in depth here.
- Pay signals suggest a field-service or reliability lead costs about $110K-$160K base, and a remote shift operator costs roughly $20-$35/hour depending on geography (Nomagic PLN rate, Phantom Auto 2022 USD rate). These are rough and mixed in date.

### Gaps
- Hospitals, food service, agriculture, retail and sidewalk delivery beyond Serve/Starship/Coco: not searched.
- No direct evidence that any buyer has paid a named outside vendor for site adaptation, demonstrations or remote assist in 2025-2026.
- No deadline penalty or quantified cost of delay found.
- Dexterity, Ambi Robotics, Plus One pricing, Covariant post-Amazon, Locus, Bear Robotics, Diligent (Moxi) and UR integrator pricing: not resolved by this search.
